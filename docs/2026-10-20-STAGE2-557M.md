## Findings

1. **Continued pretraining clearly helps the losses.** 0.9B tokens bring the 557M's unseen English to 28.7 (from
   32.9), close to the 168M's 27.1 despite its embedding head start, and its domain losses after the identical
   domain stage improve again (docs 23.8 → 21.8, Q&A 9.92 → 9.36).
2. **The benchmark effect of the extra tokens is within noise.** With the pilot SFT: generalization and traces up
   (20/18/4 vs 18/16/3; 29 vs 24), dev lexical and outputs slightly down (30 vs 34; 3 vs 5). Differences of 2-5 items
   on 24-90 item sets with one seed cannot separate these; the loss probes are the more reliable signal here.
3. **Size advantage holds under both SFT recipes.** Under mixed SFT the 557M (either token count) beats the 168M
   control on most columns; under the pilot SFT it did so in stage 1.
4. **Mixed SFT is a trade-off, not an upgrade.** It preserves domain and English losses far better than the pilot
   SFT, and is best on applied repairs (33/48, 10 with correct output), but dev lexical matches collapse (≈11 vs 30+)
   because the 4B answer style is long and misses the short-answer rubric; the 0.9B mixed model also never states
   "no change needed" correctly (0/24). Equal replay is too much; a 10-25% replay share is the next thing to try.
5. **The chained 168M models still lead on dev lexical, stated outputs and working programs.** Their advantage now
   looks like curriculum (many targeted SFT rounds) rather than size; the 557M has not yet been given an equivalent,
   well-tuned SFT.
